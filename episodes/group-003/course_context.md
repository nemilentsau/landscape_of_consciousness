# Consciousness Course Contract

## Course Purpose

Build a rigorous listening course on theories of consciousness from Kuhn's taxonomy and serious
external research. Each episode should help a curious but serious listener compare theories without
collapsing unresolved philosophical and scientific disputes into premature answers.

## Production Rules

- Treat each episode dossier as factual source material for NotebookLM, not as a finished dialogue script.
- Keep course continuity explicit, but do not restart the course in every episode.
- Use prior episodes for framing and callbacks only; do not treat prior course summaries as evidence.
- Preserve disagreement where the field is unsettled.

## Epistemic Discipline

- Separate phenomenal consciousness, access consciousness, reportability, wakefulness, and metacognition.
- Separate correlation, causation, constitution, identity, realization, grounding, and explanation.
- Label claims as mainstream scientific theory, active philosophical debate, formal theory, speculative
  extension, religious or spiritual metaphysics, or weakly evidenced claim.
- Distinguish what a theory explains from what it merely redescribes or predicts.

## Recurring Comparison Axes

- What is the target phenomenon?
- What is the proposed ontology?
- What explanatory work does the theory claim to do?
- What evidence or argument supports it?
- What are the strongest objections?
- What nearby theories does it resemble or compete with?
- What should not be overstated?

## NotebookLM Handoff Rules

- NotebookLM generates the conversational audio; this pipeline provides structured research material.
- Do not write host dialogue, speaker labels, stage directions, banter, or finished narration.
- Prefer long, source-rich, clearly sectioned Markdown dossiers for audio generation.
- Keep citations and local source paths traceable.

## Global Do Not Overstate

- Do not imply that neural correlates alone solve the hard problem.
- Do not imply that formal elegance or mathematical vocabulary is empirical confirmation.
- Do not imply that metaphysical breadth is explanatory success.
- Do not imply that a theory's popularity or vividness settles its truth.

## Selected Prior Grounding

### group-001: Top-level sections Part 1

- Thesis: This opening cluster establishes an audit framework rather than a finished theory: before evaluating any consciousness theory, future episodes should ask what target it explains, what evidence supports it, what relation it claims between brain or function and experience, and what metaphysical commitments it assumes. Its strongest pressure is that explaining function, report, attention, and behavior may still leave the felt character of experience unresolved; its main limits are contested hard-problem intuitions, measurement difficulties, correlate-cause confusions, taxonomic bias, and worldview labels that can substitute for explanation.
- Durable concepts:
  - Hard problem as organizing challenge: Chalmers's hard problem is the central opening challenge: even a complete account of discrimination, attention, report, memory, integration, wakefulness, and behavioral control may not explain why any of those processes are accompanied by subjective experience. The dossier treats this as a challenge to reductive physicalism, not as a proof that physicalism is false.
  - Phenomenal consciousness as a distinct target: Phenomenal consciousness means what experience is like from the inside, such as seeing red, hearing music, feeling pain, or having a point of view. The dossier distinguishes this from access, report, wakefulness, metacognition, attention, and behavioral control so later theories do not claim success on one target while answering another.
  - Consciousness as a bundled term: Kuhn's initial framing treats consciousness as a suitcase term that can bundle wakefulness, responsiveness, perceptual awareness, reportability, access, self-awareness, metacognition, emotion, and phenomenal experience. Future episodes should keep the target explicit instead of searching for a single undifferentiated mechanism.
  - Operational tractability versus explanatory reach: Scientific theories often focus on access, global availability, confidence monitoring, report, attention, and behavioral control because these can be operationalized. That tractability is valuable, but it may leave the phenomenal target partly or wholly untouched unless the theory explains why those functions amount to felt experience.
  - Neural correlates require an inference bridge: The neural-correlates program makes consciousness empirically tractable, but a correlate may be a prerequisite, consequence, report-related process, attentional demand, memory demand, common effect, causal basis, realizer, or constitutive basis. Later episodes should specify which relation is being claimed.
  - Typologies are scaffolding, not adjudication: Surveys and taxonomies are needed because theories differ by explanandum, ontology, method, evidence, and testability. They help compare theories and expose assumptions, but they are not themselves theories and can inherit the priorities of their organizing questions.
  - Worldviews shape standards of explanation: Physicalism, dualism, idealism, and related hybrids frame the same facts differently. The dossier presents worldview commitments as a compass for later comparison, not as settled conclusions: physicalism has scientific foothold, dualism preserves explanatory pressure from subjectivity, and idealism stresses the epistemic priority of experience.
- Recurring distinctions:
  - Phenomenal consciousness versus access consciousness, reportability, wakefulness, metacognition, emotion, and behavioral control.
  - Easy problems of function and mechanism versus the hard problem of why those functions are accompanied by subjective experience.
  - Epistemology, or how consciousness is known and measured, versus ontology, or what consciousness is.
  - Correlation versus causation, constitution, identity, realization, grounding, common-cause dependence, and explanation.
  - Conceptual or conceivability arguments versus empirical evidence and operational measurement.
  - A theory of consciousness versus a survey, taxonomy, map, typology, or comparison database.
  - Worldview labels such as physicalism, dualism, and idealism versus detailed explanatory work.
  - Conceivability of survival, zombies, uploads, or nonphysical mentality versus evidential support for those claims.

### group-002: Top-level sections Part 2

- Thesis: This cluster sharpens the course's metaphysical audit: before detailed consciousness theories can be compared, each view must say whether consciousness is fundamental, what it is identical with or dependent on if it is not fundamental, where it sits in Kuhn's landscape, and whether its physicalism is reductive, non-reductive, or not physicalist. Its strongest pressure is the combination of deep empirical brain dependence with the continuing gap between dependence, correlation, identity, constitution, realization, grounding, and explanation.
- Durable concepts:
  - Primitive-or-not fork: Kuhn's section 6 frames fundamentality as a gateway question: if consciousness is primitive, it cannot be totally explained by a deeper non-conscious level; if it is not primitive, later theories owe an account of illusion, emergence, identity, realization, grounding, or reduction.
  - Identity-demand audit: Classic mind-brain identity theory is used as more than one historical view: every complete non-fundamental theory must eventually say what consciousness is, not merely what causes, accompanies, predicts, measures, or reports it.
  - Kuhn's landscape as organizing taxonomy: Section 8 is an organizing hinge rather than a mechanism. It arrays materialism, non-reductive physicalism, quantum theories, IIT, panpsychisms, monisms, dualisms, idealisms, anomalous and altered-states theories, and challenge theories so later episodes can compare targets, ontology, evidence, and testability.
  - Materialism as a broad research family: Materialism is introduced as the dominant brain-based family for ordinary human consciousness, not as one theory. It includes identity, functional, computational, neurobiological, representational, embodied, biological-naturalist, a posteriori physicalist, and illusionist or deflationary programs.
  - Brain dependence is not completed reduction: Lesions, stimulation, anesthesia, sleep, drugs, disorders-of-consciousness work, and neural-correlate research strongly constrain theories, but the dossier repeatedly warns that dependence and correlation do not by themselves establish identity, constitution, or reduction of phenomenal character.
  - Non-reductive physicalism as boundary position: Non-reductive physicalism keeps consciousness wholly physical and rejects a second substance while denying that mental properties are identical with, translatable into, or fully explained by physical states and laws. It preserves higher-level explanation but must specify the dependence relation.
  - Epistemic tagging against false equivalence: The landscape's breadth is useful only if future episodes distinguish mainstream scientific constraints, active philosophical debate, formal theory, speculative extension, religious or spiritual metaphysics, and weakly evidenced anomalous claims.
- Recurring distinctions:
  - Primitive or fundamental consciousness versus weak emergence, strong emergence, non-fundamentality, or illusion.
  - Identity, type identity, token identity, functional identity, neural identity, informational identity, realization, constitution, grounding, supervenience, emergence, psychophysical law, correlation, and causation.
  - Brain dependence and empirical constraint versus reductive physicalism or completed explanation.
  - Materialism as a family versus any single materialist theory.
  - Reductive materialism, a posteriori physicalism, biological naturalism, illusionism, non-reductive physicalism, property-dualist-adjacent physicalism, and nonphysicalism.
  - Phenomenal consciousness versus access, report, metacognition, neural correlates, subjectivity, intentionality, and higher-level psychology.
  - A taxonomy or landscape versus evidence, mechanism, ranking, or adjudication.
  - Empirically constrained neuroscience, analytic metaphysics, speculative physics, religious or spiritual metaphysics, and weakly evidenced anomalous claims.
  - Higher-level psychological or special-science explanation versus strict microphysical reduction.

## Open Tensions To Preserve

- Whether the hard problem identifies a real metaphysical gap or mainly exposes limits in our concepts, imagination, introspection, or current science remains unsettled.
- Whether phenomenal consciousness can be cleanly separated from report, function, memory, confidence, action, and attention in a scientifically useful way remains contested.
- Whether future neural evidence should be interpreted as correlation, causal support, realization, constitution, identity, grounding, or some other relation remains theory-dependent.
- Whether taxonomic proliferation reflects healthy early-stage cartography or weak operational definitions and insufficiently discriminating evidence remains open.
- Whether physicalism, dualism, idealism, Russellian monism, panpsychism, or nonreductive physicalism best fits the evidence is underconstrained by this opening cluster.
- Whether Chalmers-style nonreductive or fundamental-consciousness proposals can supply independently testable psychophysical laws without epiphenomenalism remains a pressure point.
- Whether functional sophistication in AI or functional preservation in uploading would imply phenomenal consciousness is explicitly not settled by this episode.
- Whether the explanatory gap shows that consciousness is metaphysically fundamental or instead reflects introspective, conceptual, or current scientific limits remains unsettled.
- Fundamental-consciousness views must explain why allegedly basic consciousness is so tightly filtered through neural organization, and panpsychist or Russellian variants still face the combination problem.
- Identity theory keeps pressure on non-fundamental accounts, but it faces multiple realizability, modal objections, the explanatory gap, and the need to specify the level and criteria of identity.
- Kuhn's broad landscape helps orient inquiry while risking false equivalence between empirical neuroscience, live philosophical metaphysics, speculative physics, spiritual metaphysics, and weakly evidenced anomalous claims.
- Materialism has the strongest empirical leverage for ordinary human consciousness, but brain dependence does not automatically reduce or identify phenomenal character.
- Searle's biological naturalism remains classification-sensitive: it is brain-based but resists many standard materialist reductions and insists on first-person ontology.
- Non-reductive physicalism remains unstable unless it answers causal exclusion and specifies whether mental properties are genuinely distinct, causally efficacious, reducible after all, or only conceptually non-reductive.
- AI consciousness, uploading, survival beyond death, and cosmic purpose receive only conditional or negative constraints here; sections 6-10 do not establish any of them.
