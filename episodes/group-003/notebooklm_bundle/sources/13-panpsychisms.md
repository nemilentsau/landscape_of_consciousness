# Section 13: Panpsychisms

## Kuhn Review Anchor
- Section: 13. Panpsychisms
- Pages: 73-73
- Taxonomy placement: Panpsychisms

## Research Question
If consciousness cannot be transparently derived from wholly nonconscious matter, should we say that mind, experience, or proto-experience goes all the way down into nature's basic furniture?

## Core Claim
Panpsychism is a family of views, not one settled theory. Kuhn's anchor presents its broadest claim: physical ultimates, whether particles, fields, energy-matter, or perhaps spacetime itself, have phenomenal or proto-phenomenal properties, so mentality is a primitive feature of reality rather than a late product of lifeless matter. Contemporary analytic panpsychists usually do not claim that electrons think, rocks feel pain, or quarks have beliefs. The serious versions more often claim that extremely simple experiential ingredients, proto-experiential ingredients, or intrinsic natures belong to fundamental physical reality and that animal consciousness appears only in organized aggregates under special conditions. Key variants include panexperientialism, panprotopsychism, constitutive micropsychism, emergentist panpsychism, Russellian panpsychism, cosmopsychism, and religious or cross-cultural forms such as Vedantic, Saiva, Buddhist, and Kabbalistic panpsychist or cosmopsychist readings. The central ambition is to keep consciousness inside nature while avoiding both reductive physicalism's explanatory gap and Cartesian dualism's interaction problem.

## Strongest Case
The strongest case is a two-part metaphysical argument. First, the hard problem and anti-emergence arguments say that subjective experience is not intelligibly generated by rearranging purely nonexperiential structure and function. If consciousness is real, and if brute radical emergence is unsatisfying, then it may be less ad hoc to suppose that experiential or proto-experiential character is already present in basic reality and becomes complex in organisms. Second, the Russellian intrinsic-nature argument says that physics tells us a great deal about mathematical, structural, causal, and dispositional relations, but not the categorical nature of matter in itself. Consciousness is the one intrinsic nature we know directly from the inside, so panpsychism proposes that the hidden categorical nature of matter is at least partly consciousness-involving. Chalmers frames Russellian panpsychism as a possible synthesis: physicalism is attractive because of causal closure, dualism is attractive because it respects experience, and panpsychism tries to capture both by identifying phenomenal character with the intrinsic side of the physical. This is why the best panpsychism is not an anti-science doctrine. It accepts the public equations of physics, then adds a metaphysical interpretation of what their relata are intrinsically. Cross-cultural material, especially Vedantic and Saiva cosmopsychist traditions, also broadens the menu of options by asking whether the fundamental conscious reality might be whole-first rather than particle-first.

## Best Objections And Limits
The best objections are severe. The combination problem asks how many micro-experiences or microsubjects could compose one unified human subject; subject-summing looks unlike ordinary physical composition, because putting many points of view near each other does not obviously create a new point of view. Cosmopsychism reverses the direction but inherits a decombination problem: how does one cosmic consciousness segment into many bounded finite subjects? Nagasawa argues that constitutive Russellian panpsychism reaches a cognitive dead end: even if it is true, we may be unable to specify what microphenomenal properties are or how they aggregate into macrophenomenal consciousness. A physics objection, pressed by Sean Carroll, is that if mental ultimates add new causal influences, they threaten well-tested physics; if they add no new influences, it is unclear what explanatory work they do. A methodological objection is that panpsychism has few distinctive empirical predictions and can look like relabeling the mystery rather than solving it. A boundary objection, associated in Kuhn's anchor with Searle, asks why consciousness comes in discrete subjects if mentality is spread through nature. Finally, religious or mystical variants may be philosophically rich but should not be presented as scientific evidence for panpsychism; quantum-consciousness or cosmic-consciousness claims often become fringe or weakly evidenced when they move from metaphysical interpretation to alleged physical proof.

## Credibility / Epistemic Status
Epistemic status: active philosophical debate for analytic panpsychism, Russellian monism, panprotopsychism, and cosmopsychism; speculative extension when the claim is that actual particles, fields, spacetime, or the cosmos possess phenomenal or proto-phenomenal properties; religious/spiritual metaphysics for Kabbalistic, Vedantic, Saiva, Buddhist, panentheistic, or mystical forms; fringe/weakly evidenced claim when marketed as established physics, quantum proof, or empirical science. Panpsychism is a serious minority position in contemporary philosophy of mind, with strong proponents and strong critics. It is not a mainstream scientific theory of consciousness, has no accepted empirical measure of micro-experience, and does not currently compete with neuroscientific theories at the level of testable mechanisms.
