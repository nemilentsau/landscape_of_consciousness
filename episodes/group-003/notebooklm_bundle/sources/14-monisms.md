# Section 14: Monisms

## Kuhn Review Anchor
- Section: 14. Monisms
- Pages: 77-77
- Taxonomy placement: Monisms

## Research Question
If reality is ultimately one, can consciousness and the physical world be explained as aspects, parts, or appearances of that one reality rather than as two fundamentally separate domains?

## Core Claim
Kuhn's Monisms section is an umbrella category rather than a single testable mechanism. The anchor is Schaffer's metaphysical definition: monism attributes oneness to reality, and in the strict existence-monist form says there is exactly one concrete object, with everything else identical to it or derivative from it. For consciousness theory, the relevant move is broader: whatever ultimately exists must include, ground, or express both what we call physical and what we call mental. That makes monism a crossroads between physicalism, panpsychism, neutral monism, Russellian monism, dual-aspect theories, idealism, anomalous monism, reflexive monism, and theological or spiritual one-reality views. The family resemblance is anti-Cartesian and anti-gap: do not put mind and matter into two fundamentally alien substances, and do not explain consciousness by simply omitting or deflating phenomenal experience. The price is that 'monism' by itself is too thin. A serious monist theory must still say what the One is, how finite subjects arise within it, why mental and physical descriptions both apply, and whether the proposal adds explanatory power beyond relabeling the hard problem.

## Strongest Case
The strongest case for monism combines parsimony with integration. Substance dualism respects phenomenal consciousness but faces interaction, pairing, and causal-closure problems: how does an immaterial mind affect a physical brain, and why this brain rather than another? Reductive materialism fits well with neuroscience but appears to leave an explanatory gap between complete structural, functional, or neural descriptions and what experience is like from the inside. Monist theories try to keep one world while avoiding both failures. Schaffer's priority monism gives the whole-first version: perhaps the cosmos is the one fundamental concrete object and parts are derivative, a view motivated by questions about quantum holism, atomless gunk, intrinsicness, and law-governed unity. Russellian monism gives the consciousness-focused version: physics tells us relational and dynamical structure, but not the intrinsic nature of the entities bearing that structure; consciousness is the one intrinsic reality we know directly, so phenomenal or protophenomenal properties may be the categorical base of physical reality. Neutral and dual-aspect monisms soften that claim by saying the underlying reality is neither simply mental nor simply physical, while mind and matter are two aspects, organizations, or descriptions of it. Davidson's anomalous monism offers a different, physical-ontology version: mental events are physical events, but psychological explanation is not reducible to strict physical law. At its best, monism is a disciplined attempt to make consciousness belong to nature without making nature merely third-person mechanism.

## Best Objections And Limits
The best objections start with category confusion. Monism about how many concrete objects are fundamental is not automatically a theory of consciousness; the claim that the cosmos is basic does not explain why there are pains, colors, thoughts, or unified subjects. Existence monism is especially hard to square with ordinary and scientific talk about many concrete things, while priority monism must defend controversial assumptions about dependence, fundamentality, and whether the whole really explains its parts. Consciousness-focused monisms have their own problems. Russellian and panpsychist versions face the combination problem: many micro- or proto-phenomenal ingredients do not obviously compose one bounded human point of view. Neutral monism must specify the neutral base without letting it collapse into disguised materialism, idealism, or dualism; otherwise 'neutral stuff' becomes a placeholder. Dual-aspect monism can sound like a verbal truce unless it gives rules linking the two aspects and explains why brain changes track experience so tightly. Davidson's anomalous monism is vulnerable to the charge that mental properties become causally idle: if the event causes behavior only under its physical description, what causal work does mentality do? Physics-based objections press harder: if consciousness adds new causal influence, where does it enter tested physics; if it adds none, what explanatory work has been gained? Finally, many spiritual, cosmic-mind, Jungian, or quantum-flavored monisms may be philosophically rich but become weakly evidenced when promoted as empirical science.

## Credibility / Epistemic Status
Epistemic status: active philosophical debate for analytic monisms such as priority monism, Russellian monism, neutral monism, anomalous monism, dual-aspect monism, and reflexive monism; speculative extension when the view claims that the cosmos as a whole, fundamental physics, information, quantum structure, or mathematical state space explains consciousness; religious/spiritual metaphysics when the One is God, divine mind, absolute consciousness, or a spiritually purposive universe; fringe/weakly evidenced claim when monism is advertised as established neuroscience, proven quantum physics, or direct empirical evidence for cosmic consciousness. Monism is not a mainstream scientific theory of consciousness in the way global workspace, higher-order, recurrent processing, predictive processing, or integrated information theories function as empirical research programs. It is best treated as a live metaphysical framework that can constrain or interpret such theories, not as a substitute for neural, computational, behavioral, and clinical evidence.
