# Section 9.2: Neurobiological theories

## Kuhn Review Anchor
- Section: 9.2. Neurobiological theories
- Pages: 19-19
- Taxonomy placement: Materialism theories -> Neurobiological theories

## Research Question
If consciousness is a brain phenomenon, what would actually explain it: a firing pattern, a recurrent circuit, a global broadcast, a posterior hot zone, a connectome pathway, an electric field, or some causal capacity revealed only when the brain is perturbed?

## Core Claim
Kuhn's section 9.2 is an umbrella for materialist theories that start from known brain mechanisms rather than from abstract functional roles alone. The family claims that conscious states depend on, are realized by, caused by, or constituted by specific neurobiological organization: neuronal transmission, recurrent and long-range circuits, thalamo-cortical and cortico-cortical pathways, connectome structure, electrical dynamics, and the neural correlates of conscious contents and conscious level. It should not be treated as one theory. Edelman's reentry and dynamic core, Crick and Koch's NCC program, Baars and Dehaene's global workspace, recurrent-processing accounts, posterior-hot-zone proposals, attention-schema and attention-based theories, temporo-spatial dynamics, and clinical perturbational-complexity measures all sit near this terrain but disagree about the target: phenomenal consciousness, reportable access, perceptual contents, wakeful level, self-modeling, attention, integration, or flexible control.

## Strongest Case
The strongest case is empirical and mechanistic. Consciousness changes systematically with neural injury, anesthesia, sleep, seizures, stimulation, neuromodulation, and disorders of consciousness; these are not loose correlations but interventions that alter what can be experienced, reported, remembered, or behaviorally used. NCC research has also produced increasingly sharp contrasts: masking and binocular rivalry compare similar sensory input with different awareness; no-report paradigms try to separate conscious perception from reporting demands; TMS-EEG work shows that waking cortex can sustain complex, distributed causal responses while deep sleep or anesthesia can collapse responses into local activity; perturbational complexity can help classify conscious level without depending solely on external behavior. The 2024 multiscale review is important because it suggests that rival neural theories may partly describe different mechanistic levels rather than simply contradicting one another. The 2025 Cogitate adversarial collaboration further raises the bar: neurobiological theories are now being forced into preregistered, shared tests rather than only post hoc interpretation.

## Best Objections And Limits
The central objection is that neural dependence and neural correlation are not yet a constitutive explanation of experience. A brain signal can track consciousness, enable consciousness, precede consciousness, follow consciousness, or support report and memory without being the thing that makes an experience feel like anything. The field is also underdetermined: fronto-parietal ignition, local sensory recurrence, posterior hot zones, higher-order monitoring, integrated information, attention schema, and temporo-spatial dynamics can sometimes reinterpret the same data in different vocabularies. Methodologically, many experiments still depend on reports, confidence ratings, attention, working memory, and task demands; no-report paradigms reduce that problem but introduce their own inference risks. Philosophically, Chalmers-style hard-problem worries and Block's phenomenal/access distinction remain pressure tests: a theory may explain access, discrimination, control, or report while leaving phenomenal character underdescribed. Finally, the umbrella can become too elastic unless each theory states its target phenomenon, necessity and sufficiency claims, scope limits, and risky predictions.

## Credibility / Epistemic Status
Epistemic status: mainstream scientific theory as a broad empirical research family, with active philosophical debate about whether any current neurobiological account explains phenomenal consciousness rather than only its neural conditions, contents, access, or reports. Specific mechanisms vary in credibility: NCC mapping, anesthesia/sleep/lesion evidence, causal perturbation, and global or recurrent circuit models are serious mainstream science; claims about which exact network is necessary and sufficient remain contested. Extrapolations to AI consciousness, mind uploading, or connectome-only identity are speculative extensions unless tied to explicit biological or computational criteria. This section does not require religious/spiritual metaphysics, and claims that a single brain feature or field pattern by itself solves consciousness should be marked as weakly evidenced unless it survives causal and comparative testing.
