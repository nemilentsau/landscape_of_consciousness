# Section 9.4: Computational and Informational Theories

## Kuhn Review Anchor
- Section: 9.4. Computational and Informational Theories
- Pages: 28-28
- Taxonomy placement: Materialism theories -> Computational and Informational Theories

## Research Question
If mind is information processing, what extra, if anything, turns the right computation into something it is like to be?

## Core Claim
Kuhn's section 9.4 is an umbrella within materialist theories: consciousness is explained, realized, or at least scientifically modeled by advanced computational organization and information dynamics. The family includes symbolic and connectionist computational theories, neural and cognitive architectures, resonance systems such as Adaptive Resonance Theory, complex adaptive systems, critical-brain models, information-theoretic measures, holonomic or frequency-domain proposals, and other mathematical models. Its shared wager is not that every ordinary calculation is conscious, but that conscious states depend on physically realized causal-information organization: how signals are transformed, integrated, broadcast, stabilized, predicted, synchronized, resonated, and used for flexible control. The section should therefore be presented as a broad research strategy rather than one finished theory. It overlaps with global workspace, predictive processing, IIT, AI consciousness, and mathematical/criticality work, but Kuhn's taxonomy separates some of those when they become distinctive enough to deserve their own packets.

## Strongest Case
The strongest case is that computation and information give consciousness science a common mechanistic language connecting brain activity, cognition, behavior, and artificial systems. Turing, Putnam, Fodor, Marr, and later computational neuroscience made it plausible that mental capacities can be described at levels of function, algorithm, representation, and implementation rather than only by the chemical identity of neurons. Chalmers gives the philosophical version: if mentality is an organizational invariant, then the right causal-computational structure could be sufficient for mind, and computation is a powerful way to specify that structure. Neuroscience gives the empirical version: sensory neurons carry information, unconscious and conscious processing can be distinguished by access, recurrence, global availability, metacognition, and report, and current theories routinely formulate their claims in information-processing terms. ART links consciousness to resonant states that stabilize attention and learning; criticality work links conscious states to near-critical cortical dynamics that may maximize flexible information flow; and AI-consciousness indicator reports show how recurrent processing, global workspace, higher-order monitoring, predictive processing, and attention-like control can be translated into architecture-level checks. At its best, this family is rigorous because it forces theories to say what operations are necessary, what architecture implements them, what evidence would count, and why the same conscious capacity might be realized in more than one physical substrate.

## Best Objections And Limits
The central objection is that computation and information may explain access consciousness, report, discrimination, learning, and control without explaining phenomenal consciousness itself. Levine's explanatory gap and Chalmers's hard problem still apply: after a complete information-processing story, critics can ask why those operations feel like anything from the inside. A second objection is overbreadth. If computation is defined too loosely, almost any physical system can be interpreted as implementing some computation; if information is defined only in Shannon-style terms, it tracks uncertainty reduction without automatically supplying meaning, subjectivity, or value. Piccinini and Scarantino's distinction between computation and information processing matters here: the two slogans are not interchangeable. Searle's Chinese Room presses the syntax-versus-semantics challenge: running a program may not by itself produce understanding or experience unless the implementing system has the right causal powers. Block's absent-qualia and China-brain cases press the same worry against functional equivalence. Biological and embodied critics add that metabolism, affect, interoception, sensorimotor coupling, development, timing, analog dynamics, and living self-maintenance may be constitutive rather than optional implementation details. Methodologically, information-theoretic and dynamical measures can be useful correlates while still failing to identify the difference-makers of consciousness; many theories remain underdetermined by the same data. Finally, AI and mind-uploading extrapolations are high-risk: a system can be computationally fluent, mathematically complex, or resonant in some sense without that alone establishing consciousness.

## Credibility / Epistemic Status
Epistemic status: mainstream scientific theory as a broad modeling and research framework in cognitive science, neuroscience, computational psychiatry, AI, and consciousness science; active philosophical debate as a sufficient metaphysical account of phenomenal consciousness. The weak claim that conscious cognition involves information processing and can often be modeled computationally is highly credible. The stronger claim that the right computation or information dynamics alone are sufficient for subjective experience is unresolved. Specific subtheories vary: global-access and recurrent-processing models are mainstream empirical programs; ART and criticality models are serious but more specialized; holonomic and broad mathematical proposals need careful separation from speculative extensions. Applications to machine consciousness, mind uploading, or digital immortality are speculative extensions. The view is not religious or spiritual metaphysics unless it is merged with cosmic-information or survival claims. Claims that information as such, any sufficiently complex system, or any resonance automatically has experience are fringe or weakly evidenced unless they supply independent criteria and empirical tests.
