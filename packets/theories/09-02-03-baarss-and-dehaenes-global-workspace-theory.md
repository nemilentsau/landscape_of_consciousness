# Baars’s and Dehaene’s global workspace theory

## Course Role
This packet supports a long-form listening-course episode on What turns a mental representation into a conscious one: its local neural strength, or its ability to be used by the whole mind?

- Section: 9.2.3. Baars’s and Dehaene’s global workspace theory

- Taxonomy placement: Materialism theories -> Neurobiological theories -> Baars’s and Dehaene’s global workspace theory

## Core Claim
Global Workspace Theory (GWT), and Dehaene's Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT), claim that a content becomes conscious when it wins competition among parallel unconscious processors and is amplified into a broadly available workspace. The conscious content is not a private inner observer or a single brain spot; it is the temporarily dominant information package that can be used by perception, attention, memory, valuation, verbal report, and action systems. In the neural version, conscious access is associated with recurrent amplification or 'ignition' across long-range cortico-thalamic and fronto-parietal/sensory networks.

## Strongest Case
The theory explains a central contrast in cognition: the brain performs many specialized operations unconsciously and in parallel, while conscious experience is limited, serial, reportable, and flexible. Baars's cognitive architecture gives a functional reason for this: a global broadcast lets many specialized systems coordinate around one currently important content. Dehaene and Naccache then turned that architecture into a testable neuroscience program. In masking, attentional blink, and intracranial EEG studies, unseen stimuli often produce early local processing, while consciously perceived stimuli are associated with later, more sustained, more distributed activity, long-distance synchrony, gamma-band changes, and access to report, memory, and voluntary control. The 2020 Neuron review presents GNWT as a mature empirical framework linking conscious contents, conscious states, attention, working memory, and anesthesia. The 2025 Cogitate adversarial collaboration also found some results compatible with GNWT, including conscious-content information in inferior frontal cortex and content-specific synchronization between frontal and early visual areas.

## Best Objections
The deepest objection is that GWT may explain access consciousness rather than phenomenal consciousness: it identifies what can be reported, remembered, and used, but critics ask whether that is the same as what it is like to experience. This creates a report-confound problem, because many experiments track consciousness through report or task behavior, and frontal activity may reflect decision, metacognition, working memory, or report rather than experience itself. Recurrent processing theorists argue that local recurrent activity in sensory cortex may be enough for visual experience, with global broadcasting only making contents available for cognition. Posterior-hot-zone and IIT-oriented critics similarly challenge the necessity of prefrontal cortex for specifying conscious contents. The strongest current empirical pressure comes from the 2025 adversarial test: it found evidence for some broadcasting, but GNWT was challenged by limited representation of some conscious dimensions in prefrontal cortex and a general lack of predicted ignition at stimulus offset. The right conclusion is revision pressure, not falsification of the whole workspace idea.

## Credibility Notes
Epistemic status: mainstream scientific theory with active philosophical debate. GWT/GNWT is one of the leading materialist neurobiological theories of consciousness and has a large empirical literature. Its strongest support concerns conscious access, reportability, flexible control, and differences between conscious and unconscious processing. It remains unsettled whether global availability is constitutive of phenomenal consciousness or is instead a downstream access, report, or control mechanism.

## Listener Hooks
- The theater metaphor works only if the 'stage' is distributed and has no inner spectator; otherwise it collapses into the Cartesian theater picture GWT rejects.
- Masked-word studies: a word can be processed enough to influence behavior without being consciously seen, but reportable awareness appears when processing becomes stronger, sustained, and more widely connected.
- Attentional blink: the same kind of stimulus can be missed or consciously seen depending on whether it triggers the late global ignition.
- Cocktail-party competition: many streams are processed, but only one wins center stage and becomes broadly usable across memory, speech, and action.
- The adversarial-collaboration hook: rival consciousness theorists agreed in advance on tests, and the results forced both GNWT and IIT to absorb uncomfortable data.
- AI question: a global workspace architecture may be functionally implementable in machines, but GWT alone does not settle whether such access would produce phenomenal experience.
