# Surveys & typologies

## Course Role
This packet supports a debate-club episode on What does a map of consciousness theories buy us before we try to choose a winner?

- Section: 4. Surveys & typologies
- Pages: 5-5
- Taxonomy placement: Surveys & typologies

## Core Claim
Surveys and typologies are not themselves a theory of consciousness. Their core claim is methodological: because 'consciousness' spans experience, awareness, access, reportability, creature-state distinctions, metaphysical status, and neural mechanisms, the field needs explicit maps before it can compare explanations. Kuhn's anchor uses Bayne's definitional strategies, Chalmers' Type A-F metaphysical taxonomy, PhilPapers' bibliographic categories, Seth and Bayne's neurobiological survey, and Horgan's idiosyncratic mind-body portraits to show that consciousness research is a many-level landscape rather than a single linear contest. The best version treats typologies as scaffolding for comparison: they expose which question a theory answers, what ontology it assumes, what evidence would count for or against it, and where rival theories may be talking past one another.

## Strongest Case
The strongest case is that consciousness research badly needs this kind of cartography. SEP-style overviews distinguish concepts of consciousness, philosophical surveys separate metaphysical projects from mechanistic projects, and current neuroscience reviews compare higher-order, global workspace, re-entry/predictive processing, and integrated information approaches by their explanatory targets and empirical commitments. Chalmers' Type A-F scheme clarifies the reductive versus nonreductive metaphysical options; PhilPapers shows the scale and structure of the philosophical literature; Signorelli, Szczotka, and Prentner argue that theories differ in their explanatory profiles; Francken and colleagues show that active researchers still disagree about definitions and methods; Yaron and colleagues' ConTraSt database demonstrates why quantitative, theory-neutral comparison is needed. In that sense, typology is a progress-enabling tool: it turns a confusing pile of theories into explicit questions about scope, mechanism, ontology, evidence, and testability.

## Best Objections
The main objection is that maps can look more neutral and exhaustive than they really are. A taxonomy inherits the biases of its starting questions: Chalmers' scheme centers the hard problem and metaphysical entailment; neurobiological surveys center mechanisms and measurable neural correlates; Horgan's pluralism centers lived subjectivity; Kuhn's landscape arrays theories along a roughly physicalist-to-nonphysicalist spectrum. These are useful choices, not discoveries of nature's own joints. A second objection is underdetermination: the proliferation noted by Seth and Bayne may indicate productive early science, but it may also show that current data and operational definitions are too weak to rule theories out. Doerig, Schurger, and Herzog argue that empirical theories need harder criteria; ConTraSt found that support for a theory can track methodological choices rather than findings; the 2025 Cogitate adversarial test of IIT and GNWT challenged important claims on both sides rather than cleanly crowning a winner. Typologies can organize disagreement, but they do not by themselves solve the hard problem, the measurement problem, or the problem of deciding which explanandum matters most.

## Implications
- AI consciousness: Not central unless this theory gives explicit criteria for machine consciousness.
- Virtual immortality: Not central unless this theory treats substrate, identity, or mind uploading directly.
- Survival beyond death: Not central unless this theory includes nonphysical survival claims.
- Meaning/value: Not central unless this theory explicitly connects consciousness to value or purpose.

## Credibility Notes
Epistemic status: active philosophical debate plus mainstream scientific meta-methodology. Surveys and typologies are highly credible as orientation tools, literature maps, and frameworks for comparing theories, especially when they are transparent about scope and criteria. They have low credibility as a substantive explanation of consciousness by themselves. Kuhn's ambition of a near-exhaustive landscape is useful as a heuristic, but should be presented as a curated map rather than a proven universal taxonomy.

## Listener Hooks
- The airport-map hook: before arguing about the destination, show listeners that the field has different terminals for metaphysics, neuroscience, phenomenology, computation, and spirituality.
- The Type A-F hook: Chalmers turns the mind-body problem into six lanes, from eliminative or reductive materialism to dualism and Russellian-style monism.
- The proliferation hook: in mature sciences, evidence often narrows the field; in consciousness science, major surveys report that theories have multiplied instead.
- The adversarial-collaboration hook: the 2025 Nature test put IIT and global neuronal workspace theory into the same experimental ring and found pressure points for both.
- The Horgan hook: make the abstract taxonomy human by asking why experts' views about consciousness often connect to their fears, hopes, biographies, and ideas of the self.
